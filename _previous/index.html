Research

Fraud detection and algorithmic fairness, summer 2026
Advised by Richard Torres Molina, Whitman Department of Computer Science

Fraud models are judged on how much fraud they catch. I also wanted to know who they wrongly flag.

I built a stacked ensemble classifier on the synthetic Sparkov transaction dataset, comparing random forests, XGBoost and neural networks. Because fraud is rare, I evaluated with precision, recall and precision-recall curves rather than accuracy. I then audited the models across cardholder groups using statistical parity, equalized odds and equal opportunity.

  • Preprocessing and feature engineering in Python with pandas, NumPy and scikit-learn, including log transforms for skewed features like city population
  • A literature review covering Kozodoi et al. (2022), Kamalaruban et al. (2024), Hardt et al. (2016) and the Bank Account Fraud dataset suite
  • Methods sections of a technical paper on fraud-injection calibration, preprocessing and skewness analysis
